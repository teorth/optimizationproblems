# Ambidextrous Moving Sofa Constant

## Description of constant

The ambidextrous moving sofa constant $C\_{41b}$ asks for the maximum area of a sofa, as defined in $C\_{41a}$ that can navigate both left and right corners inside a Z-shaped corridor of width 1, where the corners are sufficiently far apart.
That is, the shape of the largest area (the "sofa") that can be moved from one end of the corridor to the other by a continuous rigid motion (translation and rotation).
Trivially, $C\_{41a} \ge C\_{41b}$

## Known upper bounds

| Bound | Reference | Comments |
| ----- | --------- | -------- |
| $2 \sqrt{2}$ | [Hammersley1968] | Intended for $C\_{41a}$, but can be modified for $C\_{41b}$ |
| 2.2195 | [Baek2024] | Naive bound from $C\_{41a}$ |
| $2\sqrt{2}-1 \approx 1.8284271$* | [Pat2026] | Hammersley-type argument using both turns. At the moment each turn has rotated by $45^\circ$, the sofa lies in the strip and in the left and right corridors turned by $45^\circ$. In coordinates $s=x-y$, $t=x+y$ the strip becomes the band $0\le t-s\le 2$ and the two corridors become axis-aligned L-shapes of arm width $\sqrt2$ opening in opposite directions. A four-case analysis (two $\sqrt2$-squares, plus a column or row whose width trades off against the squares) bounds the triple intersection by $2\sqrt2-1$; a turn of less than $45^\circ$ leaves area below $\sqrt2$. Uses the standard formulation (monotone rotation by at most $90^\circ$ in each turn, as in Kallus–Romik). The bound is the exact value of this one-angle relaxation, attained by two hexagons. |
