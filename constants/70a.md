# Reverse Brunn-Minkowski constant

## Description of constant

For subsets $K,L\subset\mathbb{R}^n$, their Minkowski sum is

$$
K+L\ :=\ \{x+y:\ x\in K,\ y\in L\}.
$$

In general, one cannot expect a reverse Brunn-Minkowski inequality for arbitrary compact sets, even with a fixed multiplicative constant.
<a href="#Bas1995-no-general-reverse">[Bas1995-no-general-reverse]</a>

Milman's reverse Brunn-Minkowski theorem says that for centrally symmetric convex bodies, after putting the bodies in a suitable relative position, one does get a dimension-free reverse inequality.
<a href="#Bas1995-milman-statement">[Bas1995-milman-statement]</a>

More precisely, define $C_{\mathrm{RBM}}$ as the infimum of constants $C>0$ such that for every $n\ge 1$ and every pair of centrally symmetric convex bodies $B_1,B_2\subset\mathbb{R}^n$, there exists a linear map $u$ with $\lvert\det u\rvert=1$ satisfying

$$
\lvert u(B_1)+B_2\rvert^{1/n}\ \le\ C\bigl(\lvert B_1\rvert^{1/n}+\lvert B_2\rvert^{1/n}\bigr).
$$

<a href="#Bas1995-milman-statement">[Bas1995-milman-statement]</a>

We define

$$
C_{70}\ :=\ C_{\mathrm{RBM}},
$$
the reverse Brunn-Minkowski constant.

Milman's theorem implies $C_{\mathrm{RBM}}<\infty$.
<a href="#Bas1995-milman-statement">[Bas1995-milman-statement]</a>

A trivial lower bound is $C_{\mathrm{RBM}}\ge 1$.

Including the unrefereed finite certificate below, the recorded range is

$$
1.20194336\ \le\ C_{70}\ <\ \infty.
$$

## Known lower bounds

| Bound | Reference | Comments |
| ----- | --------- | -------- |
| $1$ |  | Trivial scaling lower bound. |
| $1.201927$ | [Pat2026] | Cube $B\_1=[0,a]^n$ and ball $B\_2=rB$ with $n=300000$, $a/r=0.013$. For $\lvert\det u\rvert=1$, $u(B\_1)$ is a parallelotope; its intrinsic volumes are sums of $j$-face volumes, and Szász's inequality on its Gram matrix with AM–GM gives $V\_j(u(B\_1))\ge\binom{n}{j}a^j$, so by Steiner's formula $\lvert u(B\_1)+rB\rvert\ge\sum\_j\binom{n}{j}a^j\kappa\_{n-j}r^{n-j}$ for every $u$. Hence $C\_{70}\ge(\sum\_j\binom{n}{j}a^j\kappa\_{n-j}r^{n-j})^{1/n}/(a+\kappa\_n^{1/n}r)$; evaluated in interval arithmetic. ($n=2$ gives $\sqrt{1/2+1/\sqrt\pi}\approx1.0316$; the value increases with $n$.) |
| $1.20194336$* | [Pin2026] | Optimization of Patankar's cube-and-ball construction. Retain the Steiner term with $n=10^{12}$, $k=367879441172$, $t=k/n$ and $a/\kappa\_n^{1/n}=(n-k)/k=632120558828/367879441172$; its normalized root is enclosed near $1.20194336844315056699>1.20194336$. Arb and exact-rational logarithm/Stirling checkers, both written by the same AI agent, pass. The paper proves the single-term limit $\exp(1/(2e))>1.20194336847$ as even $n\to\infty$ and $k/n\to1/e$; this analytic limit is separate from the finite certificate. Unrefereed, not formally verified, without independent human review or an optimality claim. |

## Additional comments and links

- The finite certificate gives $C\_{70}>1.20194336$; the prose limiting argument gives the stronger $C\_{70}\ge\exp(1/(2e))>1.20194336847$. The asterisk records the self-archived, unrefereed verification status. The construction and positioning reduction are due to Patankar; this note optimizes a single term.

- **Original source.** The reverse inequality was introduced by Milman in 1986.
  <a href="#Mil1986">[Mil1986]</a>

- **Proof history.** Bastero-Bernues-Pena explicitly record that Pisier (1989) and Milman (1988) gave alternative proofs of Milman's theorem.
  <a href="#Bas1995-proof-history">[Bas1995-proof-history]</a>

- **Published extension.** Bastero-Bernues-Pena prove a broader affine-invariant reverse inequality for bodies with $p(A_1),p(A_2)\ge p$ (with constant depending only on $p$).
  <a href="#Bas1995-extension-theorem1">[Bas1995-extension-theorem1]</a>

- [Wikipedia page on Milman's reverse Brunn-Minkowski inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milman%27s_reverse_Brunn%E2%80%93Minkowski_inequality)
