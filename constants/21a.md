# de Bruijn–Newman constant

## Description of constant

Define

$H(\lambda, z):=\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{\lambda u^{2}} \Phi(u) \cos (z u) \, du$,

where $\Phi$ is the super-exponential decaying function

$\Phi(u) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (2\pi^2n^4e^{9u}-3\pi n^2 e^{5u} ) e^{-\pi n^2 e^{4u}}.$

Newman showed in [N1976] that there exists a finite constant $C_{21}$ (the de Bruijn–Newman constant) such that the zeros of $H$ are all real precisely when $\lambda \geq C_{21}$.

## Additional comments

- **Asterisks on the post-2021 rows.** The five pre-existing post-2021 entries [MI2026], [MI2026b], [Kor2026], [MI2026c] and [MI2026d] are self-archived certificate packages on Zenodo or a personal repository, not refereed and not independently replayed, so all five carry the marker under the rule in [CONTRIBUTING.md](https://github.com/teorth/optimizationproblems/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md). The six additional rows retain the marker; no removal is requested. Planat reports reproducing the frozen finite comparison for $0.149396$ [Pl2026a, §8] and both $0.116398$ histories [Pl2026b, App. A]; these numerical replays do not independently validate the complete analytic proofs. The last refereed bound is $0.2$ [PT2021]. The three $0.16$-range packages all descend from the $0.1875$ row, which they take as their starting point, so they are not independent of it.
- **The $7/8$ input.** The rows for $0.13963$, $0.116398$ and $0.107964910079$ use the statement that every nontrivial zero of $\zeta$ has real part at most $7/8$, stated in OpenAI's preprint dated 30 September 2026 [OAI2026] and formalised as `OAI.riemannZeta_ne_zero_of_seven_eighths_lt_re` in the pinned Lean development [OAI2026L]. The source is unrefereed. This page follows the existing [65a](https://teorth.github.io/optimizationproblems/constants/65a.html) convention for the $2.4$ bound deduced from the same source: an asterisked upper bound with its input named. The marker also covers that input, and the original conditional formulations of [HB2026] and [Ray2026c] remain explicit. Planat's (A1)–(A3) identify the imported Raymond source, localisation and global-contact arguments, sharing the analytic foundation of the $0.149396$ row; the $7/8$ theorem is a separate input to the rows that name it. Numerical replay does not independently validate that analytic foundation. The best refereed bound is $0.2$ [PT2021]. None of the three new $7/8$ comparison arguments is claimed here to be formalised end to end.
- The Riemann hypothesis holds iff $C_{21} = 0$, so the lower bound likely cannot be improved upon.
