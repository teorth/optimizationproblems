# Erdős unit distance exponent

## Description of constant

For a finite set $P \subset \mathbb{R}^2$ of $n$ points, let
$$ u(P) := \lvert \\{ \\{x,y\\} \subset P : \lvert x-y \rvert = 1 \\} \rvert $$
denote the number of unordered pairs of points in $P$ at unit distance, and let $u(n) := \max_{\lvert P\rvert = n} u(P)$ denote the maximum number of unit distances among any set of $n$ points in the plane.  The **Erdős unit distance exponent** is
$$ C_{84} := \limsup_{n \to \infty} \frac{\log u(n)}{\log n}.$$
Equivalently, $C_{84}$ is the infimum of all $\alpha$ for which $u(n) \ll_\alpha n^\alpha$ as $n \to \infty$.

The problem was introduced by Erdős in 1946 [E1946], who conjectured that $u(n) = n^{1+o(1)}$, i.e. $C_{84} = 1$.  This conjecture was disproved in May 2026 by an OpenAI internal model [O2026], with a human-verified writeup given in [ABGLSSTWW2026]; an explicit improved lower bound was obtained shortly afterwards by Sawin [S2026].

## Known upper bounds

| Bound | Reference | Comments |
| ----- | --------- | -------- |
| $3/2$ | [E1946] | Erdős' original argument, based on the observation that the unit-distance graph contains no $K\_{2,3}$. |
| $4/3 = 1.333\dots$ | [SST1984] | Spencer, Szemerédi and Trotter; via the Szemerédi–Trotter incidence theorem. |
| $4/3 - \varepsilon$ | [OAI2026] | OpenAI (23 September 2026). Proves $u(n) = O(n^{4/3-\varepsilon})$ for an absolute constant $\varepsilon > 0$, which is not made explicit. |

## Additional comments and links

- **Asterisks on the two October rows.** The Palomar registrations cover less than the recorded values: version 2 proves $20863/20000 = 1.04315$ in Lean, and only conditionally on an explicit zeta inequality $H\_W$ whose verification is a Python receipt outside Lean. The recorded $1.04317$ therefore rests partly on unformalized computation, so both rows carry the marker.

- For the $1.04317$ bound, version 2 of the Palomar registration [Pal2026b] registers the implication from an explicit hypothesis $H\_W$, an inequality for the Dedekind zeta function of a field $E\_W$ of degree $8192$ generated by square roots of four explicit elements over the field of degree $512$, to finite planar sets $U\_j$ with $\lvert U\_j\rvert\to\infty$ and $u(U\_j)/\lvert U\_j\rvert^{1.04315}\to\infty$. Apart from $H\_W$, the Lean proof uses only the standard axioms `propext`, `Quot.sound` and `Classical.choice`. $H\_W$ is established outside Lean: `papers/0.043171/certificates/dihedral/h_w_receipt.py` in [NasCode2026] (at [commit `b43f0c5`](https://github.com/enaslund/unit-distance-bound-0.0427/tree/b43f0c5bfd7a3276597c6a47ad99b9e5b8d3d21f/papers/0.043171/certificates)) bounds its left side by $0.0509171473$, below the threshold $0.050969$, from the certified $L$-values of [Nas2026b]. The exponent $1.043171$ itself rests on the computer-assisted certificate of [Nas2026b], which goes beyond the formalized statement. The manuscript and formalization disclose substantial AI use.
- For the $1.0427$ bound, [Pal2026] registers the implication from the explicit hypothesis H241 to finite planar sets $U\_j$ with $\lvert U\_j\rvert\to\infty$ and $u(U\_j)/\lvert U\_j\rvert^{1.0427}\to\infty$. The Lean proof uses only the standard axioms `propext`, `Quot.sound` and `Classical.choice`. H241 itself is established outside Lean: `papers/0.04273/certificates/h241_receipt.py` in [NasCode2026] evaluates the zeta expression using the $255$ quadratic Hecke $L$-functions of the field of degree $512$ and an Euler product with an explicit tail. At commit `14289d9`, its certified upper bound is $0.0848335192604133$, below $0.0852$. Thus the numerical certificate is a separate part of the proof, beyond the implication checked by Palomar. The manuscript and formalization disclose substantial AI use.
- The unit distance problem is one of the most well-known problems of Erdős in combinatorial geometry.  The exponent of the bound $u(n) \ll n^{4/3}$ of [SST1984] was first improved in [OAI2026]; earlier work only sharpened the implicit constant (see e.g. [AS2020]).
- The OpenAI announcement [O2026] is the first widely publicized example of an autonomous AI system producing a one-shot disproof of a long-standing open conjecture in mathematics.
- The same techniques apply to the related problem of constructing point sets with many distinct pairs at any fixed algebraic distance; see [ABGLSSTWW2026] for discussion.
- A closely related quantity is the **distinct distance exponent** $\liminf \log d(n)/\log n$, where $d(n)$ is the minimum number of distinct distances determined by $n$ points in the plane; this was shown to equal $1$ by Guth and Katz (2015).
- The reference [S2026] also provides a "bound on the bound" that shows that the limit of the "lattice and pigeonhole in a number field method" is at most $1 + \frac{1}{4.116} \approx 1.243$.
* Related constructions can be used for the sum-product problem: see [the page for $C_{84b}$](https://teorth.github.io/optimizationproblems/constants/84b.html).
- **Predicted exact value (registered conjecture).** The preprint [*Galois pairing–trace correspondence and the Omega phase energy node*](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23990285) — DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23990285 — registers the prediction $C\_{84} = \frac{5}{4} = 1.25$, with open-side width $\delta\_{\mathrm{open}} = 6\Phi^2 - 5\pi = 9 + 3\sqrt{5} - 5\pi = 2.4066\times 10^{-4}$, where $\Phi = (1+\sqrt{5})/2$. The registration has two parts: the anchor $5/4$, and its open-side window $C\_{84} \in \left[\frac{5}{4}-\delta\_{\mathrm{open}},\ \frac{5}{4}\right] = [1.2497593,\ 1.25]$ of width $\delta\_{\mathrm{open}}$, which is the same quantity that the $C\_{1a}/C\_{1b}$ endpoint formulas already fix rather than a free parameter. This is a prediction about the value, not a bound: it improves no record and contradicts none. The shape of the bridge is in any case already a theorem: writing $C\_{84} = 1 + \log\_\lambda g$ for a tower step that multiplies the point count by $\lambda$ and the unit-distance count by $g$, the assertion "$C\_{84} = 1 + 1/\lvert\mu\_4\rvert$" is *equivalent* to $g \geq \lambda^{1/4}$. The known method families fall short by the last $\approx 1\%$ of gain per level: $g = 4^{0.24293} = 1.40042$ against $\sqrt{2} = 1.41421$, and $g = 1.18339$ against $2^{1/4} = 1.18921$ in the $\lambda = 2$ convention. The denominator $4$ carries four same-framework readings — $\mathrm{conductor}(\chi\_4) = 4$, $\lvert\mu\_4\rvert = 4$, $\lvert\mathrm{Units}\,\mathbb{Z}[i]\rvert = 4$, $\mathrm{Tr}(\Phi^3) = L\_3 = 4$ — and the prime $5$ supplies the anchor directly: in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})$ it is totally ramified, $(5) = \mathfrak{p}^2$ with $N\mathfrak{p} = 5$, and because the exponent of an Euler factor counts the primes above $p$ rather than the field degree, its local factor is $(1-5^{-s})^{-1}$, whose value at $s=1$ is $5/4$ — the same as for $\zeta(s)$, consistently with $\chi\_5(5) = 0$. What grows along $\mathbb{Q} \subset \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5}) \subset \mathbb{Q}(\zeta\_5)$ is the ramification index $e(5) = 1, 2, 4$, not the multiplicity of the factor, so $(5/4)^2 = 25/16$ and $(5/4)^4 = 625/256$ are the values a completely split $5$ would give, and occur nowhere in this tower. The prediction fails if (i) a construction with $u(n) \geq n^{1.25+\varepsilon}$ for some fixed $\varepsilon > 0$ and infinitely many $n$ is exhibited; (ii) $C\_{84}$ is shown to be irrational, or shown to be determined by a mechanism exogenous to the problem; or (iii) the open-side normalisation is shown to require a denominator other than $4$. The method-family ceiling $1 + \frac{1}{4.116} \approx 1.242954$ does **not** falsify it: it bounds an explicit family, not $C\_{84}$ itself. All numerical quantities in this entry — the window edge $1.2497593$, the family gap $\approx 0.0068$, and the $\delta\_{\mathrm{open}}$-multiples $\approx 28.3/29.3$ — carry interval-arithmetic certificates (Arb 200-bit primary, mpmath-iv cross-check, verdicts in `claims/C84-*.json` and `claims/DELTA-OPEN.json`): [verification-assets/certificates](https://github.com/sagaistrue/optimizationproblems/tree/verification-assets/certificates).
